The California Administrator's Playbook: Special Education Compliance & Leadership

California's dated obligations, monitoring system, and dispute exposure — what is due, when, in this state

Education Across the States · Charles Mayfield, Producer Current through November 5, 2026 — next scheduled review July 2027

How to use this playbook

Compliance failures in special education are rarely knowledge failures. They are calendar failures and verification failures: a date nobody owned, a report somebody assumed was clean. This playbook is built to prevent both. Section 1 is California's compliance calendar for the 2026-27 school year, taken from the state's own published documents — every date carries its recurring rule and its source, so your team can rebuild the calendar when the state moves dates (states move dates; the July re-verification sweep exists for that reason). Sections 2–5 cover monitoring, dispute exposure, staffing, and corrective action. Section 6 is the closing discipline: what leadership must verify in primary records rather than accept from summaries.

Labeling convention: obligations that are genuinely federal are marked (federal). Everything else is California's own.

State assessment windows. California's statewide testing (including the alternate assessment for students with the most significant cognitive disabilities) runs each spring on windows CDE sets annually. This playbook does not print a window it cannot tick to a stored current-year CDE calendar: pull the current year's testing calendar from CDE when building site schedules, and confirm that IEP assessment accommodations are loaded from the IEPs themselves — not from last year's extract — before the window opens.

Leadership translation: the dated calendar (1A) is roughly a dozen deadlines a year. The rolling calendar (1B) is thousands of deadlines a year, one per student-event. Your compliance risk lives in 1B; your monitoring exposure (Section 2) is computed from how 1B performed.

Section 2 — State monitoring and indicators

The SPP/APR framework as California publishes it

California's State Performance Plan is a six-year plan built on the indicator set the U.S. Office of Special Education Programs (OSEP) requires of every state; the Annual Performance Report reports results against it each year. As CDE publishes them, the Part B indicators are:

  1. Graduation · 2. Dropout · 3. Statewide assessments (participation and proficiency) · 4. Suspension/expulsion (4B — significant discrepancy by race/ethnicity — is a compliance indicator) · 5. Least restrictive environment · 6. Preschool LRE · 7. Preschool outcomes · 8. Parent involvement · 9. Disproportionate representation (identification) · 10. Disproportionate representation by disability category · 11. Child Find — timely initial evaluation (compliance) · 12. Part C to Part B transition — IEP in effect by the third birthday (compliance) · 13. Secondary transition — transition plans meeting requirements (compliance) · 14. Post-school outcomes · 17. State Systemic Improvement Plan (SSIP).

Two structural facts from CDE's own overview page drive management attention:

  • Compliance indicators carry absolute targets — 0% or 100%. Indicators 4B, 9, 10, 11, 12, and 13 are not "improve by a few points" measures. A single late initial evaluation is an Indicator 11 miss; a single noncompliant transition plan is an Indicator 13 finding. Performance indicators (graduation, LRE, outcomes) carry state-set targets; compliance indicators carry the law.
  • CDE publishes LEA-level APRs, and LEAs that miss targets may be required into monitoring activities. Your indicator profile is public and precedes you into every community conversation.

Annual Determination Letters and CIM

Each year CDE issues Annual Determination Letters (most recently, early February 2026 for the prior cycle). Per CDE's CALPADS guidance (Flash #314), the letter's timeline-compliance section is computed from CALPADS data — including Monitoring Report 16.21's analysis of late annual plan reviews and late triennial reevaluations against the Census Day snapshot. LEAs identified through this process complete Compliance Improvement Monitoring (CIM) activities with CDE.

What this means operationally: your determination is substantially written by your own data entry. Before each certification in §1A, run the LEA's annual-review and triennial due-date reports from the same CALPADS extracts the state will use, reconcile them against case-manager records, and fix discrepancies in the source systems. A determination letter should never be the first place leadership learns an annual was late.

Disproportionality watch

Indicators 4B, 9, and 10 make discipline and identification patterns by race and ethnicity a standing compliance exposure, not an annual surprise. Pull your suspension/expulsion and identification rates by subgroup at every CALPADS certification, and treat divergence as a Section 5 corrective-action trigger before the state does.

Pattern recognition for leadership: state complaints cluster on implementation (services in the IEP not delivered) and timelines (the §1B clocks). Due process clusters on placement and eligibility judgment calls. The first cluster is a systems problem you can engineer away; the second is a documentation-and-relationship problem you manage case by case. Read your own complaint history through that split before buying any other theory.

Section 4 — Staffing and caseload

  • The one hard number: a resource specialist teacher's caseload shall not exceed 28 pupils (EC §56362). Audit assignments against it every term — it is statutory, it is checkable, and it is exactly the kind of fact that surfaces in a complaint investigation.
  • Most other California caseloads are local decisions — set by SELPA local plans, district policy, and collective bargaining, not by a statewide cap. That makes them your numbers to defend: know each program's ratio, know which ratios are contractual, and document the analysis when you exceed your own targets. "The state doesn't cap it" is not a rationale at a hearing; a reasoned, documented staffing analysis is.
  • Fall 2 is your staffing mirror. The February 26, 2027 CALPADS Fall 2 certification reports staff assignments and FTE as the state counts them. Reconcile Fall 2 against your HR system and your service logs before certification: discrepancies among the three are how a routine data submission becomes a credibility problem.
  • Credentialing pinch points to verify, not assume: assessor qualifications for the evaluations your assessment plans promise (Stage 2 of the educator playbook runs on licensed/certificated assessors in each area), interpreter availability for meetings and assessments in families' native languages, and substitute coverage for service providers — a service missed for want of a sub is still a missed service in a compensatory-education analysis.
  • Vacancy math: an unfilled special education position is not a savings line; it is accrued compensatory-services exposure with interest paid in attorney time. Report vacancies to the board in those terms.

Section 5 — Corrective action: how findings close

When CDE (through a complaint investigation, a determination, or CIM activities) or an OAH decision finds noncompliance, the sequence is consistent:

  1. Finding — stated against a specific requirement, on a specific record.
  2. Corrective action — student-level remedy first (compensatory services where a student was denied FAPE), then system-level change (procedures, training, staffing), each with an owner and a date.
  3. Evidence of completion — documentation the verifying body can check: corrected data, revised procedures, service logs showing delivery.
  4. Verification and closure — CDE verifies before it closes. A corrective action reported complete on assertion alone stays open.

Leadership rules that make this painless: never dispute a finding your own records confirm — fix it and bank the credibility for the finding you genuinely contest. Track every corrective action in one register with owners and evidence links. And close the loop into Section 1: most findings are §1B clocks that no system caught; the corrective action that doesn't change the underlying calendar system is a down payment on the same finding next year.

Section 6 — Verify, don't assume: the leadership checklist

Before each CALPADS certification and each board report, personally confirm — in the primary system, not in a summary:

  • ☐ Fall 1 / Fall 2 / EOY extracts reconcile to the student information system and to case-manager records (special education counts, staff FTE, discipline incidents)
  • ☐ Annual-review and triennial due-date report pulled; every overdue item has a name and a recovery date (this data becomes Report 16.21 in your determination letter)
  • ☐ Assessment-plan log: every referral in the period produced a plan within 15 days, or carries a documented break-exclusion calculation
  • ☐ Consent-to-IEP log: every initial completed within 60 days of consent, or documented exclusion
  • ☐ Records-request log: every request answered within 5 business days
  • ☐ IEE request log: every request funded or filed on, without unexplained delay
  • ☐ Service-delivery sampling: a random pull of IEPs against provider logs shows minutes delivered as written
  • ☐ RSP caseloads at or under 28 (EC §56362); all other ratios at or under your own adopted targets, or documented exceptions
  • ☐ SELPA local plan calendar: CAC review, public hearing, COE approval, and the June 30 CDE submission all calendared with owners
  • ☐ Complaint and due-process register current: every open matter has an owner, a next deadline from §1B/§3, and a status you have seen yourself
  • ☐ 504 register: coordinator named, plans current with review dates, every >10-day disciplinary removal preceded by reevaluation
  • ☐ Determination letter and CIM obligations (when issued) entered into the corrective-action register with evidence requirements

This playbook is general administrative information, not legal advice. District counsel reviews contested matters; CDE's published calendars control when dates move.
