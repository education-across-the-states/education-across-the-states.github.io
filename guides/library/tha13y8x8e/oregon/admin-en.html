The Oregon Administrator Playbook

Compliance leadership for special education in Oregon — the calendar first, then the systems behind it

Education Across the States · Charles Mayfield, Producer Current through November 5, 2026 — next scheduled review July 2027

This playbook's calendar (§1A) contains only dates verified against Oregon Department of Education publications stored with this guide. Where Oregon publishes a pattern rather than a fixed date, §1C says so plainly instead of inventing one. Oregon's full district Schedule of Due Dates is published by ODE for registered district users; reconcile this calendar against it each August.

Why December is the whole game. The December Child Count is not a headcount ritual — it is the district's funding and accountability spine. The SECC feeds the State School Fund's special education weighting (each SECC-counted child generates 2.0× the basic support amount — the "IEP weight" — up to 11% of the district's resident average daily membership, per the SECC Manual) and supplies the child-count inputs for the State Performance Plan indicators. ODE's own warning, from the manual, deserves to be quoted in every business office: each district "will be considered not Timely or Accurate if even one submission is late or inaccurate," and the district becomes "Needs Intervention" for that school year. One bad submission by one building, one time, is a district-level status for a year.

§1B — Rolling clocks (no fixed calendar date; the clock starts on an event)

These obligations have no place on a wall calendar because a family's action — or your district's — starts them. They are where Oregon districts actually get found noncompliant.

§1C — Annual patterns (real obligations; Oregon publishes no single fixed date in the sources behind this guide)

  • Annual LEA determinations. Under the federal framework Oregon implements, districts receive an annual determination (Meets Requirements / Needs Assistance / Needs Intervention / Needs Substantial Intervention) based on compliance and results (34 CFR 300.600(a)(2); ODE publishes district Special Education Profiles through its At-A-Glance profile system, with district indicator performance against state targets). A late or inaccurate December submission alone forces Needs Intervention status (see §1A).
  • SPR&I — Systems Performance Review & Improvement. Oregon's district improvement-planning system, through which districts review indicator data and build improvement plans under ODE's General Supervision system. Treat the SPR&I cycle as an annual operating rhythm anchored to your profile release and determination, not as a form to file.
  • State Performance Plan / Annual Performance Report. Oregon's SPP/APR is maintained by ODE (most current edition stored with this guide: FFY 2024). District data collections throughout the year — child count, exit, Indicator 11 — are the APR's raw material; every §1A deadline is ultimately an APR deadline.
  • Assessment windows, IDEA grant application dates, and the full collection schedule. ODE publishes a comprehensive Schedule of Due Dates for district users and the state assessment calendar through its district pages. Those schedules were not retrievable as public documents for this guide's source set (see the claims table's gap disclosure) — pull them at the start of each year and reconcile; do not run a district on this calendar alone.

§2 — The monitoring system you operate inside

Oregon's General Supervision system, run by ODE's Office of Enhancing Student Opportunities (OESO), is built on the eight components of OSEP's Differentiated Monitoring and Support framework: state performance plan, data on processes and results, the integrated monitoring activities, fiscal management, dispute resolution, technical assistance and professional development, policies/procedures/practices, and improvement/incentives/sanctions (OAR 581-015-2015; ODE General Supervision page). What that means operationally:

  • Identification is continuous, not episodic. Noncompliance is identified through monitoring reviews, data analysis, dispute-resolution outcomes (complaints and hearings feed supervision), and district self-assessment. Your complaint orders and your data anomalies are the same file.
  • The correction standard is strict. ODE notifies in writing within 3 months of identification; the district must correct as soon as possible and in no case later than one year after notification (OAR 581-015-2015(2)–(3)). Correction means verified: the district implements the required action and ODE verifies the district is correctly implementing the requirement — a policy revision memo is not correction. (Federal practice's two-prong standard — child-specific remedy plus systemic verification — is how ODE applies this rule.)
  • Your public face is data. District At-A-Glance Special Education Profiles publish your SPP indicator performance against state targets, and your Timely and Accurate Data standing is a standing public fact. Boards should see the profile before the public does — every August, without fail.
  • Complaint orders are conditional funding facts. ODE's authority (OAR 581-015-2030) reaches noncompliance under the IDEA, Division 15, and ORS 343. Manage contracted providers' service delivery as your own: a complaint investigation examines the services the child actually received, wherever they were delivered, and the IEP being read is yours.

Management notes: Oregon publishes logs of every complaint and hearing request filed since 2008, and hearing decisions go to the State Advisory Council and are made public with identifying information removed (OAR 581-015-2375(4)) — your district's dispute history is a public document; manage it as one. Two levers districts underuse: the ALJ settlement conference and advisory opinion processes ODE describes — an advisory opinion gives both sides a neutral read of the case early, when settlement is still cheap. And remember the retaliation rule: retaliation against anyone for filing or participating in a complaint investigation is itself a violation (OAR 581-015-2030(19)) — make sure every building administrator in your district knows it before a complaint arrives, not after.

§4 — Staffing, caseloads, and service capacity

The honest headline: Oregon sets no statewide caseload caps in its special education rules. A review of OAR Chapter 581, Division 15 — the rules stored with this guide — contains no caseload maximums for special education teachers, case managers, or related-service providers. Caseloads in Oregon are set by district policy, collective bargaining agreements, and workload reality. That absence is itself the administrator's problem:

  • Your accountability is outcomes-based, not ratio-based. ODE will never cite you for a caseload number; it will cite you for the missed evaluations, unimplemented minutes, and blown 60-school-day clocks that excessive caseloads cause (OAR 581-015-2015; 581-015-2110). Model workload against the clocks, not against a cap that does not exist.
  • Staffing can never justify a shortened day. ORS 343.322 prohibits an abbreviated school day program based on inadequate staffing — in statute, without exception. A coverage plan that depends on sending students home early is an unlawful plan the day it is written (see §5).
  • Licensure and assignment are state functions. Teacher and administrator licensure runs through the Teacher Standards and Practices Commission (TSPC); ODE's complaints process and TSPC's are separate doors — and notably, a superintendent's failure to restore a student's full school access under the abbreviated-day statute is itself grounds for TSPC discipline (ORS 343.328).
  • The ESD is a capacity lever, not a vendor afterthought. Regional Inclusive Services and ESD-delivered programs carry low-incidence populations your district cannot staff alone; contract language should track service delivery and records duties with the same rigor as your own buildings' — the IEP, and ODE's review of it, will be read against the district that wrote it.

§5 — Corrective action and institutional recovery

When ODE finds noncompliance — through a complaint order, a due process decision, or monitoring — the recovery sequence Oregon expects is:

  1. Child-level remedy first. Complaint orders address the affected student's services, including compensatory education or monetary reimbursement where appropriate (OAR 581-015-2030(13)). Compensatory education is owed for the service failure, calculated from service logs against IEP commitments — another reason minute-level logs are a leadership system, not a teacher chore.
  2. Systemic correction on the clock. Notify → correct ASAP, no later than one year, with ODE verification (OAR 581-015-2015). Build the correction plan at identification, not at month eleven.
  3. Documented verification. Keep the evidence file — training rosters, revised procedures, sampled IEPs demonstrating correct implementation — assembled as you go. Correction that cannot be shown cannot be verified.
  4. Abbreviated-day violations have a personal edge. Failure to restore full access after consent revocation can be referred to TSPC as grounds for the superintendent's discipline (ORS 343.328) on top of the district's corrective action and ODE complaint exposure. Treat every abbreviated-day consent file as a superintendent-level record: consent on file, review meetings on schedule (25–35 days, then 30-day cycles under ORS 343.326), revocations honored immediately.
  5. Deadlines missed in December compound. A Needs Intervention determination from a single late or inaccurate submission (SECC Manual) shapes the district's public profile, its monitoring intensity, and its improvement obligations for the following year. Protect the December chain: snapshot discipline on December 1, submission by December 14, review-window work in January — staffed, calendared, and owned by a named administrator.

§6 — The verify-don't-assume checklist

Before each board report on special education, the responsible administrator confirms — from records, not assurances:

  • ☐ December Child Count reconciled: SECC submission accepted, review-window discrepancies resolved before ODE's April 15 fiscal use
  • ☐ Evaluation clocks audited: sample of files shows consent-to-eligibility inside 60 school days or a documented exception
  • ☐ Records requests logged at ≤ 10 business days to delivery
  • ☐ Every abbreviated-day student: signed consent on file, IEP-team reviews on the statutory schedule, zero students on shortened days for staffing reasons
  • ☐ Manifestation determinations completed within 10 school days for every disciplinary change of placement; cumulative removal days tracked per student
  • ☐ Complaint and due process posture current: response clocks staffed (10 business days to ODE), resolution meetings calendared (15 days), corrective actions inside their one-year outer limit with verification evidence filed
  • ☐ Determinations and At-A-Glance profile reviewed with the board before public release; SPR&I improvement actions updated from the newest data
  • ☐ 504 systems checked separately: grievance procedure published, coordinator designated, hearing-request routing understood (no stay-put — interim placement decisions are being made deliberately, not by default)

General information for Oregon administrators, not legal advice. Every date in §1A is ticked, in the claims table stored with this playbook, to the ODE publication that sets it; dates Oregon publishes only through its district portal are disclosed as gaps there rather than reconstructed here.
