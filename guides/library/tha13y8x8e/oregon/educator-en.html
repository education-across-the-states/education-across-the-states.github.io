The Oregon Educator Playbook

Special education practice in Oregon, stage by stage — for case managers, special educators, school psychologists, and related-service providers

Education Across the States · Charles Mayfield, Producer Current through November 5, 2026 — next scheduled review July 2027

Before the stages: Oregon's structure in one page

  • The law stack. ORS Chapter 343 (statute) → OAR Chapter 581, Division 15 (rules, OAR 581-015-2000–2990) → the Oregon Standard IEP form (OAR 581-015-2215), which is mandatory statewide unless ODE has approved an alternate form for your district. When practice and the form disagree, the form is telling you the rule.
  • Who delivers what. Districts serve school-age students. ESDs deliver Early Intervention/Early Childhood Special Education and, with district partners, Regional Inclusive Services for low-incidence populations. Know which agency employs the provider for any service on an IEP you manage — it determines whose records, whose supervision, and whose name is on the finding if delivery fails.
  • Oregon counts in school days where it counts most. The evaluation clock is 60 school days, not 60 calendar days (OAR 581-015-2110(5)). Build that into your referral workflow, your testing calendar, and every conversation you have with a parent about "when."
  • The signature protections. Oregon's distinctive educator-facing rules: consent mechanics with an asymmetric override (Step 2 of the Evaluate stage), records out in 10 business days (ORS 343.173), an abbreviated-day statute that prohibits unilateral shortened schedules and prohibits staffing as a reason (ORS 343.322), and a dispute system where ODE finds violations in writing and orders correction — findings that land in your district's General Supervision record.

Stage 1 — Identify

The duty. Oregon imposes an affirmative Child Find duty: districts must identify, locate, and evaluate children suspected of having a disability and needing special education (ORS 343.157; OAR 581-015-2080) — including highly mobile children, homeless children, wards of the state, private-school children, and children advancing grade to grade. Suspicion, not certainty, is the trigger; structured interventions may inform an evaluation but may not be used to postpone one once suspicion exists.

In practice.

  • A parent's written request is the cleanest trigger in the system — treat its date as day zero of your response workflow. Oregon gives you no separate statutory response clock for a referral; your clock discipline begins at consent (Stage 2), and a referral that produces no consent form and no prior written notice of refusal is a complaint waiting to be filed.
  • Document the basis of knowledge. For discipline purposes (OAR 581-015-2440), a teacher's specific concerns about a pattern of behavior expressed directly to the director of special education or other supervisory personnel create district knowledge — route behavior patterns up, in writing, not as hallway conversation.
  • For children under 5, the pathway runs through your ESD (EI/ECSE). Coordinate the kindergarten transition early; the receiving district owns the IEP, and the handoff is where evaluations stall.

Stage 2 — Evaluate

Planning first (OAR 581-015-2115). Evaluation planning is a team act, not a school psychologist's errand: the IEP team and other qualified professionals, with the parent as a participant, review existing data — evaluations and information provided by the parents are first in the rule's list — and identify what additional data, if any, the team needs on disability, present levels, need for services, and (at reevaluation) needed changes. The review may occur without a convened meeting, but if a meeting is held, the parent must be invited. The evaluation itself must address all areas related to the suspected disability (OAR 581-015-2110(2)), and the team may not use any single measure or assessment as the sole criterion (OAR 581-015-2110(4)).

Consent mechanics (OAR 581-015-2090) — learn the asymmetry.

  • Initial evaluation: informed written consent required (parent fully informed, in their native language, agreement in writing). If a parent refuses or doesn't respond, the district may — but is not required to — pursue the evaluation through mediation or due process; declining to pursue is not a Child Find violation.
  • Initial services: consent is also required, and here the district may not override a refusal or non-response — no mediation, no hearing, no services, no FAPE violation for withholding them, and no obligation to convene an IEP meeting.
  • Reevaluation: consent required; if the parent fails to respond despite documented reasonable efforts, the district may proceed without it. A refusal may be overridden through mediation or due process.
  • Revocation after services begin: written revocation stops services after prior written notice; the district may not override, need not amend the record, and the revocation ends IDEA protections wholesale.

Treating evaluation consent as services consent — in either direction — is the most common Oregon consent error. They are different signatures with different legal consequences.

The clock (OAR 581-015-2110(5)). Initial evaluation and reevaluation: 60 school days from receipt of parental consent to the meeting at which eligibility is considered. The only authorized extensions are the documented exceptions: mid-evaluation transfer with sufficient progress and a written agreement on a specific completion date; a parent who repeatedly fails or refuses to produce the child; or a written agreement extending the timeline for a specific learning disability determination. If an exception applies, it goes in the child's record — an undocumented exception is an unexcused miss.

Transfer students (OAR 581-015-2230). A student with an IEP who transfers within Oregon: the new district, in consultation with the parents, provides FAPE — including services comparable to those in the prior IEP — until it either adopts the previous district's IEP or develops, adopts, and implements a new one. From out of state: comparable services, in consultation with the parents, until the district conducts an initial evaluation if it determines one is necessary and develops and implements a new IEP. A transfer that lands mid-evaluation is governed by the clock exception in OAR 581-015-2110(5)(c)(A): the timeline survives only with documented sufficient progress and a written agreement with the parent on a specific completion date.

Eligibility (OAR 581-015-2120). The team — parents plus two or more qualified professionals, at least one knowledgeable and experienced with the suspected disability — prepares an evaluation report and a written statement of eligibility covering: data considered; whether the child meets a category (OAR 581-015-2130–2180; developmental delay, 581-015-2127, ages 3–9); exclusionary factors (lack of appropriate instruction in reading/math; limited English proficiency); adverse impact on educational performance; and need for special education. Each team member signs, indicating agreement or disagreement. A dissent signed is a record protected; a dissent suppressed is a finding.

ORS 343.146 quality controls. Medical, vision, and hearing examinations used in evaluation must be administered by licensed practitioners (physician/NP/PA; optometrist or ophthalmologist; audiologist). The statute requires a full and individual evaluation before the initial provision of special education — and notably sets no day-count: Oregon's timelines live in the State Board's rules, which is why this playbook cites OAR 581-015-2110 for every clock.

Stage 3 — Write

Required content (OAR 581-015-2200(1)). Present levels of academic achievement and functional performance (including how the disability affects involvement and progress in the general curriculum); measurable annual goals, with short-term objectives for students taking alternate assessments; how progress toward goals will be measured and when periodic reports issue (concurrent with report cards is the usual pattern); the special education, related services, supplementary aids, and program modifications or staff supports to be provided, based on peer-reviewed research to the extent practicable; an explanation of any nonparticipation with nondisabled children; assessment participation and accommodations (a student may not be exempted from state assessment because of a disability unless the parent requests an exemption under OAR 581-022-0612); and the projected start date with the anticipated frequency, amount, location, and duration of each service.

Drafting discipline:

  • Minutes are commitments. "30 minutes, 2× weekly, special education setting" is implementable and auditable; "push-in support as needed" is neither. Oregon complaint orders read IEPs literally — write them that way.
  • Goals measured the way they promise. If the goal says weekly probes, the file should fill with weekly probes. Progress reporting is a required IEP component, not correspondence.
  • The LRE explanation is not boilerplate. Any removal from the regular environment requires an explanation tied to this child and this goal set.

Transition (OAR 581-015-2200(2)). Beginning not later than the first IEP in effect at 16 — earlier, at 14 or younger, when the team (including the parents) determines it appropriate — include measurable postsecondary goals for training, education, employment, and where appropriate independent living, based on age-appropriate transition assessments of strengths, preferences, and interests, plus the transition services and courses of study that support them. Oregon adds a district duty practitioners miss: provide parents, at each annual review, information on supported employment services through Oregon Vocational Rehabilitation and the Office of Developmental Disabilities Services in competitive, integrated settings. Build the VR/ODDS handout into your annual-review packet; its absence is discoverable in the file. Beginning not later than one year before the student turns 18, the IEP must state that the student has been informed of the rights transfer at majority (OAR 581-015-2200(2)(b); 581-015-2325).

The meeting itself (OAR 581-015-2190, 2195). Notice must state purpose, time, and place; identify attendees; tell parents they may invite others with knowledge or special expertise; and state that the team may proceed without the parent if the parent does not attend. For transition meetings, the notice must also indicate that transition will be discussed, that the student will be invited, and identify any agency invited to send a representative (OAR 581-015-2195(4)). Schedule at a mutually agreed time and place; offer phone participation or home visits; document attempts (call records, correspondence copies, visit records) before proceeding without a parent (OAR 581-015-2195(3)). Interpreters: take whatever action is necessary for the parent to understand the proceedings (OAR 581-015-2190(3)). The parent receives a copy of the IEP at no cost (OAR 581-015-2195(5)).

After eligibility: the initial IEP meeting is due within 30 calendar days of the eligibility determination, and services begin as soon as possible after the IEP is developed (OAR 581-015-2220).

Stage 4 — Implement

Delivery is the compliance event. The IEP is implemented as written — every service, at the stated frequency, amount, location, and duration — or amended by the team. "We were short-staffed" appears in Oregon law in exactly one place: as a prohibited reason for an abbreviated day (ORS 343.322). It is not a defense to a missed service anywhere else either; ODE complaint orders treat unimplemented services as service failures with compensatory-education consequences (OAR 581-015-2030(13)).

Abbreviated school days (ORS 343.321–343.331) — the practitioner's checklist.

  • Never unilateral. Placement on an abbreviated program requires informed written parent (or foster parent) consent; without consent, the student receives meaningful access to the same hours as the majority of same-grade students in the district.
  • Never for staffing. A district may not consider, recommend, or implement an abbreviated program due to inadequate staffing. If a proposed shortened day's true cause is coverage, stop the proposal.
  • Reviews are calendar events, not intentions. IEP team review 25–35 calendar days after placement begins; then at least every 30 calendar days (and never less often than every 90 calendar days for a student with an IEP, even with consent to meet less frequently); and within 14 calendar days of a parent request (ORS 343.326).
  • Revocation is immediate business. Written revocation or written objection triggers restoration of full access; a written objection at least 14 calendar days before a new school year restores full access on day one (ORS 343.328).
  • Track it like data. Oregon's Abbreviated Day Collection counts every student with an IEP, a 504 plan, or an evaluation in progress on an abbreviated program for more than 10 school days in the July 1–June 30 reporting period. Your caseload list should reconcile to that report without effort.

Behavior infrastructure. Where behavior impedes learning, Oregon practice runs on the functional behavioral assessment and behavior intervention plan (OAR 581-015-2181 governs functional behavioral assessments and behavior support plans in the eligibility division; the manifestation process in Stage 5 requires an FBA and a BIP where the rules call for them). Build the FBA before the crisis: the discipline clocks in Stage 5 assume the file already knows the behavior's function.

Stage 5 — Monitor

Progress evidence. Measure goals on the IEP's own stated schedule and method; issue reports when the IEP says you will. When data show a lack of expected progress, the response is a team conversation and an amended plan — not a quieter graph.

Annual and triennial rhythm. Review and revise the IEP periodically and at least once every 365 days (Oregon's rule, OAR 581-015-2225); reevaluate at least every three years unless the parent and district agree in writing that reevaluation is unnecessary (federal rule, 34 CFR 300.303). The reevaluation runs on Oregon's 60-school-day clock (OAR 581-015-2110(5)(b)) — start it early enough that the eligibility meeting lands inside the window.

Records discipline. Parents may examine records at any reasonable time, and records must be provided without undue delay — never more than 10 business days from request (ORS 343.173(1); OAR 581-015-2300(3)). Route every records request to a named owner the day it arrives; "the file is with the ESD" is an explanation, not a compliance state. Note that ODE's General Supervision system reviews districts on results and compliance indicators and requires correction of identified noncompliance as soon as possible and no later than one year after written notification (OAR 581-015-2015) — your building's documentation quality is what your district's determination letter is made of.

Discipline monitoring. Track cumulative removal days per student against the pattern threshold from day one, not at the manifestation meeting: a series of removals totaling more than 10 school days in a school year that constitutes a pattern (substantially similar behavior; length of each removal; total time removed; proximity) is a disciplinary change of placement (OAR 581-015-2410), with services owed during subsequent removals (OAR 581-015-2415). Informal removals — early pickups, sent-home days — count. Log them as removals.

Stage 6 — Meet (the dispute-adjacent skills)

Prior written notice (OAR 581-015-2310; ORS 343.159). Before the district proposes or refuses to initiate or change identification, evaluation, educational placement, or the provision of FAPE, the parent receives written notice: the action proposed or refused, why, the evaluation procedures/assessments/records/reports relied on, other options considered and rejected, other relevant factors — in the parent's native language or other mode of communication unless clearly not feasible. Most defensible files are won and lost here: a crisp, honest PWN is the single highest-leverage document a practitioner writes.

The manifestation determination (OAR 581-015-2420). Within 10 school days of the decision to change placement for a code violation: the district, the parent, and relevant IEP team members (as determined by the parent and the district) review all relevant information in the file — the IEP, teacher observations, relevant information the parent provides — and answer: (1) caused by, or direct and substantial relationship to, the disability? (2) direct result of the district's failure to implement the IEP? A yes to either = manifestation: conduct or review the FBA, implement or modify the BIP, return the student to the prior placement (absent parent-district agreement or the 45-day exceptions), and — where implementation failure caused it — take immediate steps to remedy the failure. A no = same discipline as nondisabled peers, with services continuing so the student participates in the general curriculum and progresses on goals. The 45-school-day interim alternative setting for drugs, weapons, or serious bodily injury applies regardless of the manifestation answer (OAR 581-015-2425); an ALJ may order a like placement where maintaining the current one is substantially likely to result in injury (OAR 581-015-2430). Expedited challenges run fast: resolution meeting within 7 days, hearing within 20 school days, decision within 10 school days after the hearing (OAR 581-015-2445).

When it becomes a case. Know the map you are feeding: mediation is free through ODE and cannot be used to delay a hearing or complaint (OAR 581-015-2335); a state complaint brings ODE investigators and a written decision within 60 days, with corrective action — including compensatory education — for founded violations (OAR 581-015-2030); a due process request starts a 15-day resolution-meeting clock, a 30-day resolution period, and a 45-day decision clock before an OAH administrative law judge (OAR 581-015-2345–2375; ORS 343.165–343.167), with evidence not disclosed five business days before the hearing subject to exclusion. Your contemporaneous notes, dated letters, and service logs are the exhibits. Write them as if an ALJ will read them — one may.

Section 504 practice notes. Oregon's 504 layer is thin by design: the federal Part 104 standards govern evaluation (variety of sources; knowledgeable group; reevaluation before significant placement changes — not an IDEA-style manifestation determination), and Oregon adds a state hearing route before the Superintendent under IDEA hearing procedures, without stay-put and without a free verbatim record, costs on the district (OAR 581-015-2395; definitions at OAR 581-015-2390). Do not promise a family IDEA protections on a 504 plan; do not deny them the protections 504 actually carries.
