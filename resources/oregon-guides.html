Oregon IEP Guide for Parents

What it is. The complete Oregon road from "I think my child needs help" to a signed, working IEP — and the dispute ladder behind it — in plain language, with every Oregon deadline stated as Oregon actually counts it.

Who it's for. Parents and guardians of children ages 5–21 in Oregon public schools (with the birth-to-kindergarten EI/ECSE route flagged where it diverges).

What's inside (free with signup). - The evaluation, step by step: evaluation planning as a team act (OAR 581-015-2115), informed written consent — and Oregon's asymmetric consent rules (a district may seek to override a refused evaluation; it may never override refused initial services) - The 60-school-day clock — Oregon counts school days from consent to the eligibility meeting, not the 60 calendar days national guides quote — plus its three documented exceptions - Eligibility as Oregon decides it: the written statement of eligibility every team member signs - The Oregon Standard IEP (one statewide form), the 30-calendar-day meeting rule, and transition planning — starting at 16, available at 14 by team agreement, with Vocational Rehabilitation / ODDS supported-employment information owed to parents at every annual review - Records in 10 business days (ORS 343.173) — not FERPA's 45 - Disputes, Oregon's way: free ODE mediation; the state complaint (ODE decision in 60 days, reconsideration, judicial review); due process before Office of Administrative Hearings ALJs; and Oregon-only extras — the ALJ settlement conference and advisory opinion - Oregon's abbreviated-school-day law: no shortened day without your informed written consent, never for staffing, reviews on a statutory schedule, revocation at any time (ORS 343.322–343.331) - Downloads: the Oregon evaluation request letter and the IEE request letter — with the correct standard, "without unnecessary delay," no invented deadline

What's state-specific. School-day counting; the 10-business-day records rule; the standard statewide IEP; the abbreviated-day consent statute; OAH-conducted hearings; complaint reconsideration and ORS 183.484 judicial review; FACT Oregon as the designated parent center.

Oregon 504 Guide for Parents

What it is. Section 504 in Oregon — the federal civil-rights floor, plus the state layer Oregon adds that most states don't have.

Who it's for. Parents of students with disabilities who learn with accommodations rather than specialized instruction — ADHD, anxiety, diabetes, severe allergies, dyslexia, medical conditions — and IEP families checking the other road.

What's inside (free with signup). - Who qualifies: the three prongs, the broad post-2008 reading of "substantially limits," and why mitigating measures don't disqualify - The evaluation: variety of sources, a knowledgeable group — and the honest truth that Oregon sets no 504 evaluation day-count, so your dated written request is the enforceable artifact - Discipline, corrected: there is no manifestation determination under 504 — the rule is reevaluation before a significant change in placement (OCR: more than 10 cumulative exclusion days) — despite what widely used national guides claim - Oregon's 504 hearing: a written request to the State Superintendent runs under IDEA hearing procedures — with the two exceptions that change strategy (no stay-put; no free verbatim record) and district-paid costs (OAR 581-015-2395) - The full ladder: 504 coordinator → district grievance → Oregon 504 hearing → OCR (180 days) → court - Oregon's abbreviated-day law expressly covers 504 students — consent, reviews, revocation, and ODE's >10-day reporting collection

What's state-specific. The state hearing route; the abbreviated-day statute's express 504 coverage; Oregon's own rule definitions for Section 504 hearings (OAR 581-015-2390).

Oregon Educator Playbook

What it is. Oregon special education practice for the people who run it daily — organized as the work actually flows: Identify → Evaluate → Write → Implement → Monitor → Meet.

Who it's for. Case managers, special educators, school psychologists, related-service providers, and the ESD staff who deliver Oregon's regional services.

What's inside (free with signup). - Evaluation planning as a team act with the parent inside it; Oregon's consent asymmetry, stated as practice rules - The 60-school-day consent-to-eligibility clock and its only documented exceptions; transfer and comparable-services duties - Writing on the Oregon Standard IEP: measurable goals, minutes as commitments, transition at 16 (or 14), and the VR/ODDS parent-information duty at every annual review - The abbreviated-day statute as a practitioner's checklist — consent, 25–35-day first review, 30-day cycles, instant revocation handling, and the staffing prohibition - Discipline mechanics: pattern analysis, the 10-school-day MDR clock, the 45-school-day interim setting, expedited clocks - A one-card Oregon clocks table for the wall by the phone

What's state-specific. School-day counting; the standard form; records in 10 business days; General Supervision's one-year correction standard as a documentation discipline.

Oregon Administrator Playbook

What it is. Oregon special education compliance leadership, led by the calendar: the dated obligations, the rolling clocks, and the systems — monitoring, disputes, staffing, corrective action — that decide a district's standing.

Who it's for. Special education directors, superintendents, business officials, and building administrators with special education portfolios.

What's inside (free with signup). - The 2026–27 Oregon compliance calendar, dated and sourced: Private School Data Collection (Sep 24–Nov 9, 2026); the December 1 Child Count snapshot; the December 14, 2026 SECC submission; the January review window; ODE's April 15 fiscal use of the count; the July 2, 2027 Exit and Indicator 11 deadlines; the federal submission by the last Wednesday in July; the Abbreviated Day Collection year - Why December is the whole game: the SECC's 2.0 IEP weight (capped at 11% of resident ADM) and ODE's rule that one late or inaccurate submission makes the district "not Timely or Accurate" — Needs Intervention for the year - Rolling clocks: the 3-month/1-year General Supervision correction standard; the 10-business-day complaint-response duty; due process 15/30/45 - The dispute map in one table — mediation (free), complaint (60 days), due process (OAH ALJs), the Oregon 504 hearing (no stay-put), expedited discipline — with the public-log reality: Oregon publishes complaints and hearing requests back to 2008 - The staffing truth: Oregon sets no caseload caps in Division 15 — accountability runs through outcomes and clocks, and staffing is statutorily forbidden as a reason for a shortened day - Corrective action and the superintendent-level edge of abbreviated-day violations (TSPC exposure under ORS 343.328); a verify-don't-assume checklist for board reports

What's state-specific. Every date on the calendar (each one ticked to its ODE source in the package's claims table); the General Supervision/SPR&I system; the SECC funding mechanics; the At-A-Glance Special Education Profiles.
