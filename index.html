Investigations

One in Five: IEPs and 504 Plans in America

Nineteen percent of public school students — nearly one in five — now have an IEP under IDEA or a Section 504 plan. Our investigation of the newest federal data asks why: real need finally counted, or a system producing its own caseload? The verdict is mixed, the evidence is discriminating, and every number is sourced. Specific learning disability — the category weak instruction would inflate — has been shrinking for two decades, while autism, ADHD, and 504-only plans drive the growth; identification rates run from about 11% to about 21% depending on the state a child happens to live in.

Read the investigation

Restraint and Seclusion in America's Schools — the national comparison

Every district, and every school inside it. Our comparison of the federal Civil Rights Data Collection for 2023–24 covers 17,591 districts and 97,094 schools: how many students were physically restrained, mechanically restrained, or secluded, at what rate per 1,000 students, and how heavily the burden falls on students with disabilities. Districts that reported zero are shown as reported zero; districts and schools that did not report are shown as exactly that — never as zero. Drill down from the nation to a state, to a district, to the individual school, and search any school by name.

The Plan Nobody Audits — published October 23, 2026

A national special on Section 504 — the accommodation plan that almost quadrupled in fifteen years under one unchanging legal test, varies from 0% to over 30% depending on the building, and has never had a federal eligibility audit. Every claim proof-checked before air.

The Law Library

Education Law by State — the official text, all 56 jurisdictions

Every school system runs on two layers of law: the education code the legislature wrote and the administrative regulations written under it. Our library links the official text of both — plus each department of education — for the 50 states, the District of Columbia, and the U.S. territories, alongside our Regulations, Section by Section audio walkthroughs as they publish. No summaries standing in for the law: the links go to the official text, which controls.

McKinney-Vento: The Students the Law Already Covers

Our briefing on the federal homelessness education law: who qualifies (three of every four identified students are doubled-up, not in shelters), the rights every district owes — immediate enrollment, school of origin, transportation with the cross-district 50/50 default — the misunderstandings that produce audit findings and lawsuits, and the board-policy and front-office build for full compliance.

Our Standard

Sources, always. Every episode description carries a Sources section naming the documents, datasets, and reporting behind it.

Nothing invented. No fabricated quotes, no invented figures, no voices created for real people. Guests who record with us are aired exactly as they recorded — their voice, unaltered. Written answers are read verbatim.

On the record. The officials and institutions we cover are invited to respond on the record, and their responses air as given.

Corrections in the open. When we get something wrong, we fix the episode and say what changed.

About

Education Across the States is produced by Charles Mayfield, executive producer and owner — independent and self-funded, with no outside backers. Roman Greco serves as associate producer. The shows cover education the way it actually works: the federal law, the state rules, the district budgets, and the meetings where decisions get made.
