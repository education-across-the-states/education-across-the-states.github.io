Investigations

One in Five: IEPs and 504 Plans in America

Nineteen percent of public school students — nearly one in five — now have an IEP under IDEA or a Section 504 plan. Our investigation of the newest federal data asks why: real need finally counted, or a system producing its own caseload? The verdict is mixed, the evidence is discriminating, and every number is sourced. Specific learning disability — the category weak instruction would inflate — has been shrinking for two decades, while autism, ADHD, and 504-only plans drive the growth; identification rates run from about 11% to about 21% depending on the state a child happens to live in.

The Plan Nobody Audits — published October 23, 2026

A national special on Section 504 — the accommodation plan that almost quadrupled in fifteen years under one unchanging legal test, varies from 0% to over 30% depending on the building, and has never had a federal eligibility audit. Every claim proof-checked before air.

Our Standard

Sources, always. Every episode description carries a Sources section naming the documents, datasets, and reporting behind it.

Nothing invented. No fabricated quotes, no invented figures, no voices created for real people. Guests who record with us are aired exactly as they recorded — their voice, unaltered. Written answers are read verbatim.

On the record. The officials and institutions we cover are invited to respond on the record, and their responses air as given.

Corrections in the open. When we get something wrong, we fix the episode and say what changed.

About

Education Across the States is produced by Charles Mayfield, producer and owner — independent and self-funded, with no outside backers. The shows cover education the way it actually works: the federal law, the state rules, the district budgets, and the meetings where decisions get made.
