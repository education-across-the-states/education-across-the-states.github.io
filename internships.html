What it is

Education Across the States runs local internships across the country for aspiring journalists. Interns cover education where they live: school board and public meetings, in-person interviews, and research from primary sources — the reporting a national show can't do from a desk.

What you get

Published bylines on a national platform when your vetted work airs. Training in public-records and education-beat reporting. Real supervision and feedback on every submission. A portfolio of broadcast-standard work, and a completion letter from the producer at the end of a successful term.

What we require

Everything you produce is submitted to the producer for vetting — every claim checked against its source — before anything airs. Interviews are recorded only with clear consent. Nothing is ever invented: no quotes, no figures, no exceptions. Terms run one semester (12–14 weeks), hours are flexible and school-first, and the internship is unpaid and educational in structure, designed to pair with academic credit where your school offers it.

Adult students — placements through your school

If you are 18 or older and enrolled at a school or college, this internship can be built as a work-based learning placement or an internship for credit under your school's own program. Your school's coordinator approves a learning plan with us before you start; a certified educator at your school awards any credit; we co-design the plan, supervise and mentor the work, verify hours, give midpoint and final assessment input, and join the final presentation. You apply and sign for yourself — every participant must be 18 years of age or older and their own legal guardian. School-referred interns do research and production-support work — source dossiers, research notes, episode preparation — all vetted by the producer before anything airs. Ask your school's internship or work-based learning coordinator to request our school partnership packet.

How to apply

Email educationacrossthestates@gmail.com with your name, your city and state, your school or background, two writing or audio samples (class work is fine), one local education story idea from your community, and your availability. If it looks like a fit, the next step is a short call with the producer's office.
