The Records Project

The go to place for any issue having to do with education across the nation

We asked every state education agency in the country — plus the District of Columbia and the five U.S. territories — for the same core records. Everything they produce is published here, in full, free: the spending ledgers, the grant files, the monitoring reports, the complaint decisions. This is the collection, and it is open to anyone who wants to check the work — ours included.

The Education Records Project is the records desk of Education Across the States, a national news organization. Requests are filed by our producer, Charles Mayfield, under each jurisdiction's own public-records law — and the record of how each agency responds is published alongside what it produces.

What we ask for

The same core menu, of every agency, in electronic form where it exists: the check register and purchase orders; agency credit-card records; the payroll listing; adopted budgets and budget-versus-actual reports; federal grant files — IDEA Part B allocations to districts, U.S. Department of Education monitoring reports with the state's responses and corrective action plans, and determination letters; state complaint and due-process hearing decisions, as the agency keeps them; major vendor contracts; state board minutes; the agency's organizational chart; its list of record systems; and the home-education data it holds. Same questions everywhere, so the answers can be compared.

For researchers and newsrooms

This collection is built to be used. The records are public records — we claim no copyright in them, and you need no one's permission to use them for any purpose. Our register, indexes, and analysis are free to use with attribution to the Education Records Project, Education Across the States.

If you are a journalist or an academic and you publish from this collection, cite the Register ID and link the entry — that is all we ask, and it is how the next researcher finds the document. If you are planning work from a production, you are welcome (never required) to tell us at educationacrossthestates@gmail.com; we link published work that draws on the collection, because the point of asking was for the records to be read.

The register also documents how each agency behaved when asked — acknowledgments, delays, denials, and fee demands are logged as part of the record. Access is the story as much as the documents are.

Our fee protocol

Our standing rule, in every jurisdiction: the default answer to a fee is no. Every request asks up front for a public-interest fee waiver or a cost estimate before charges are incurred. A fee demand is challenged in writing first — the agency must document its statutory basis, itemize the charge, state its waiver decision in writing, and show the amount is the actual, allowable cost of production, not a deterrent to access. Fee demands, challenges, and outcomes are logged in the register, in public. As of October 25, 2026, no agency has demanded a fee on any request in this project.

Rankings, comparisons, and spotlights

As productions arrive, we normalize them — always per-pupil or per-capita, never raw totals across jurisdictions of different sizes — and publish comparisons with a full methodology note: sources, denominators, caveats, and what would change the result. A ranking we cannot defend does not publish. Where a state or district dramatically outperforms, we say so, we look at what they are doing differently, and we ask their people to explain it on the record. Where one falls far short, the documents get the same attention.

The scoreboard

On October 25, 2026, the project actioned all 56 jurisdictions in a single day: 25 formal requests sent under each jurisdiction's own records law, 8 custodian-first letters where no verified formal route is published, 15 agencies that accept requests only through their own portals (filings in preparation), and 8 held because no lawful filing route could be verified — the specific gap for each is named in the Request Register; no address was guessed.

Five states limit their records laws by residency. Those requests were filed anyway, as a national news organization, with no residency claimed — and any refusal on standing will be logged in the register as part of the record. Every request is on a statutory clock; the follow-up ladder — acknowledgment watch, deadline reminder, overdue notice, agency-head escalation, the jurisdiction's formal appeal route — runs from the register, and refusals, delays, and fee demands are documented here as they happen.
