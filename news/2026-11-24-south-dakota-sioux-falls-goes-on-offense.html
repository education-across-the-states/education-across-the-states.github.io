Sioux Falls Goes on Offense: A Formula Fight in Pierre and Cuts at Home

By Charles Mayfield · November 24, 2026

The Sioux Falls School Board met Monday night, November 23, and opened budget planning for the 2027-28 school year. General education and special education programs were both asked to show where they could cut spending, while the district holds class sizes steady. Superintendent James Nold told the board the district has already cut more than $5 million in recurring spending over the past couple of years, and said stronger sales tax collections could make this budget easier to put together.

The special education piece deserves a close look. A district can ask a program to find savings, but services written into a student's individualized program under the IDEA are entitlements, not budget lines that shrink to fit. The question in the months ahead is whether every cut special education offers leaves each student's required services, and each Section 504 support, fully intact.

The board also set the district's priorities for the 2027 Legislature. The top request in Pierre is a change to the school funding formula: state aid rising by at least 3 percent a year, moving from 3 percent or inflation, whichever is less, to 3 percent or inflation, whichever is greater. Board Vice President Gail Swenson suggested pushing the figure higher, to 3.5 percent or more, noting inflation has been running over 3 percent this year. The rest of the list: defend local control, oppose diverting public funds to private education through vouchers or education savings accounts, support a federal waiver to reform the state accountability system, and address the teacher shortage, including the Critical Teacher Needs Scholarship, loan forgiveness, tuition reduction for education majors, and alternative certification in special education.

Board member Dawn Marie Johnson summed up the shift, saying she has felt the district is always on defense and is excited to have priorities that support new policies: "It is nice knowing we are going on offense." The same day, November 23, the South Dakota Department of Education announced Kadoka Middle School and Lakeview Colony School as the state's National ESEA Distinguished Schools for outstanding achievement and growth; both will be honored at the 2027 National ESEA Conference in Louisville, Kentucky, in February. And Groton Area's $26 million bond is now verified in the district's own reporting of the unofficial count: 626 yes votes (61 percent) to 398 no, clearing the 60 percent approval required, with construction planned to begin in spring or summer 2027.

Takeaway: Watch two tracks in Sioux Falls this winter — the budget cuts each program prices out, tested against the IDEA and Section 504 services students are owed, and the funding-formula change the district takes to Pierre for the 2027 session.
