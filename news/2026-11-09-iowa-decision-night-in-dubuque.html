Decision Night in Dubuque: Two Middle Schools, a $16.8 Million Gap, and a Vote Tonight

November 9, 2026 · By Charles Mayfield, Executive Producer

HomeShowsIowaSubscribe

Tonight, Dubuque decides. The Dubuque Community School District board meets at 5:30 p.m. in the boardroom at the Forum, 2300 Chaney Road, with a task force recommendation in front of it known as Scenario Three. Beginning in the 2028–29 school year, Jefferson Middle School and Washington Middle School would close, Roosevelt Middle School would serve all sixth and seventh graders, Hempstead High School would serve eighth and ninth graders with a new career and college readiness center, and Dubuque Senior High School would serve tenth through twelfth graders.

What changed over the weekend is narrow but important. The community group asking the board to pause — DCSD: Before We Decide — told petition signers in an email, read in full in today’s episode, that its petition has been validated and placed on tonight’s agenda. The closure vote itself is still described as expected in the local preview, and we have not independently read the district’s posted agenda, so we are keeping those two facts separate.

The financial test the petition puts to the board is specific. The district says it must cut $16.8 million over four years. Scenario Three saves an estimated $4.63 million — the most of the three scenarios studied, but only part of the gap. Before a decision, the group asks the district to produce an updated fiscal 2027 budget using actuals through September 30, draft fiscal 2026 audited statements, a draft fiscal 2028 budget, independent third-party projections, and a plan for the remaining gap — which the group puts at $7.3 million. The district has already approved about $30 million in revenue bonds to expand and remodel Roosevelt, with work running from spring 2027 through August 2028.

So a yes does not fix Dubuque’s budget. It closes two buildings, commits the grade rearrangement, and starts that construction clock. A no, or a delay, leaves the same shortfall in place with one fewer tool available. One board member has said they are not comfortable proceeding until questions are answered, so a delay motion is a live possibility. If a closure is adopted, continuity becomes the question: students with an individualized education program are entitled to a free appropriate public education in their receiving building, and the same care applies under Section 504. Transportation, service minutes, and familiar staff do not transfer automatically just because a grade moves. We will report tonight’s outcome tomorrow — and if the vote does not happen, we will report that instead.

Takeaway: Tonight’s vote is not a budget fix — Scenario Three covers about $4.63 million of a $16.8 million four-year problem. Whatever the board decides at 5:30 p.m., ask what plan covers the rest, and how services for students with disabilities will follow them to their new building.
