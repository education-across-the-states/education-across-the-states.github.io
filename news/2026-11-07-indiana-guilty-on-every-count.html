Guilty on Every Count: The $44 Million Count That Closed a School

November 7, 2026 · By Charles Mayfield, Executive Producer

HomeShowsIndianaSubscribe

A federal jury on Thursday found Philip Holden, the former director of the Indiana Virtual School, guilty on all counts — seventeen counts of wire fraud and one count of conspiracy to commit wire fraud. Chalkbeat Indiana reported the verdict on Friday. Holden was responsible for student enrollment, attendance, and teacher compensation at the Indiana Virtual School and at a related school, Indiana Virtual Pathways Academy, according to the indictment.

The case was, at its center, a count scheme. The schools inflated enrollment numbers to receive per-pupil funding from the state of Indiana, and that money was then funneled to for-profit companies owned by the school’s founder and paid out to the founder, the superintendent, and others, the indictment says. The total at the center of the case is forty-four million dollars in student funding: the same per-pupil stream that pays for instruction and for services for real students, including students with disabilities, whose support under I D E A depends on counts being honest.

The case began with reporting, not with an audit. Federal charges followed a two thousand seventeen Chalkbeat investigation that found ballooning enrollment, just one teacher for every two hundred twenty-two students, and financial conflicts of interest. Lawmakers tightened funding and attendance requirements for virtual schools, and the school closed in two thousand nineteen. Holden stood trial alone: the former superintendent pleaded guilty to conspiracy to commit wire fraud in June of last year and agreed to repay about one point three million dollars, and the founder pleaded guilty the day before a joint trial was due to begin. Holden’s attorneys called him a fall guy. He is due to be sentenced on February twenty-fifth, twenty twenty-seven, and we will report that sentence when it is imposed.

The verdict landed in the same week Indiana districts learned their referendum results. Unofficial counts differ slightly by source — Chalkbeat Indiana, co-reporting with W F Y I, puts it at twenty-six of thirty-eight districts passing, while the Indiana Capital Chronicle, citing the Indiana School Boards Association, counted twenty-seven of thirty-nine — and the certified tally, due by November twentieth, settles it. In Mississinewa Community Schools, where the measure lost by seventy-one votes, the cuts start now: one of the district’s two school resource officers goes starting Monday, and next year the district plans transportation cuts affecting about one thousand two hundred students to plug about seven hundred thousand dollars of a roughly two million dollar deficit. Under the property-tax changes enacted last year, districts that lost do not get to ask voters again until November of twenty twenty-eight.

Takeaway: Enrollment counts are not paperwork — they are the funding itself. An inflated count pulls real dollars out of a system that pays for real students in real classrooms. Watch two dates: the certified referendum tally by November 20, and Holden’s sentencing on February 25.
