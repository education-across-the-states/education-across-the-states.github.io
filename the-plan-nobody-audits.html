The Plan Nobody Audits: Section 504's Quiet Quadrupling

A national special · Published October 23, 2026 · 26 minutes

Home States Shows Subscribe Resources Law Library Press Kit

▶ Play episode

This deep dive examines the fastest-growing accommodation system in American public schools: plans under Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973 held by students who do not have IEPs. Drawing on the U.S. Department of Education's Civil Rights Data Collection for 2023–24 and the leading published research, Alex and Jordan walk through what a Section 504 plan is and how its three-part eligibility test differs from the IDEA; how the "504-only" rate climbed from 1.1% of students in 2009–10 to 3.9% in 2021–22 and about 1.9 million students (4%) in 2023–24; why rates range from near double the national average in Texas and New Hampshire to below half in New Mexico and Mississippi, with individual Texas middle schools above 30% and other schools at zero; the documented skew toward White students (60% of 504-only students against 44% of enrollment) and, in the first study to link school records to IRS tax data, toward higher-income families (2.9% vs. 1.5%), even as IEPs run the opposite way; the measured response of 504 enrollment to the College Board's 2017 decision to honor school accommodations on the SAT automatically; the costs of both over-identification and under-identification; and the central evidentiary gap — no national breakdown of 504 plans by condition and no national audit of 504 eligibility has ever been published, so claims about how many plans are "unnecessary" exceed what the evidence can support. Plain-English explanations, with every number sourced and the limits of the evidence stated on air.
