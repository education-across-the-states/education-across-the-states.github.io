Reported zeros

A reported zero is a report, not a verified fact. Districts self-report, and this page does not independently verify district reports. In a review of the 2015–16 collection, the U.S. Government Accountability Office found that some of the nation's largest districts reported zeros that were incorrect (GAO-19-551R, June 2019); the GAO made no finding about any district's 2023–24 report. In 2023–24, 64% of districts reported zero — including 148 districts enrolling more than 10,000 students each. This page therefore shows three states distinctly: reported 1 or more students, reported zero students, and no valid report in the CRDC file. Across a self-reported dataset, a zero can reflect different definitions, different counting rules, incidents not captured in a count, or a district that does not restrain or seclude students; this page does not assign a reason to any district's zero, and it does not treat a reported zero as proof that no incidents occurred or as proof that any incidents did occur. State definitions and counting rules differ, so cross-state gaps reflect reporting practice as well as practice itself.

The largest districts that reported zero

The districts below are the largest in the country — more than 50,000 students each — whose 2023–24 CRDC reports list zero students restrained or secluded and zero incidents. The figures are Education Across the States' sums of each district's school-level reports to the U.S. Department of Education's Civil Rights Data Collection (CRDC) for 2023–24, shown on the basis described in the methodology notes on this page. In four of the six districts, between one and three schools did not report every practice type; the zeros below are the sums of the schools that did report, which enrolled between 99.5% and 100% of each district's students.

State law is part of the context for these figures, and it differs. Texas law provides that a school district employee may not place a student in seclusion, and defines seclusion more narrowly than the CRDC does (Texas Education Code § 37.0021). Colorado law prohibits chemical, mechanical, and prone restraint of public-school students, subject to stated exceptions (Colorado Revised Statutes § 26-20-111). Tennessee law permits isolation or restraint of a student receiving special education services only in emergency situations (Tennessee Code Annotated § 49-10-1304). A reported zero that combines restraint and seclusion therefore includes, for some districts, practices their own state law prohibits or restricts to emergencies. This page reports the combined CRDC figures as reported and does not treat any component of a zero as evidence of anything by itself.

A reported zero is a report, not a verified fact, and nothing on this page claims that any district's 2023–24 zero is wrong. One district in the table, Northside Independent School District, was also named in an earlier federal review: examining the 2015–16 collection, the U.S. Government Accountability Office reported that Northside's reported zeros for that year did not represent a count of zero incidents — the district told the Department of Education it had no way to export its data to the CRDC, and it did not file the action plan required when data are missing (GAO-19-551R, June 2019). The GAO made no finding about Northside's — or any other district's — 2023–24 report, and this page makes none. For the other five districts in the table, the GAO review of 2015–16 made no district-specific finding; in that review, nine of the ten largest districts that reported zeros were found to have reported zeros that were incorrect, which is why this page treats every reported zero, including its own calculations from the file, as a report to be confirmed rather than a verified fact.

How to read these numbers

How to read these numbers. All figures come from the U.S. Department of Education's Civil Rights Data Collection (CRDC), the federal collection covering nearly all public school districts: 2023–24 (released August 2026), with 2020–21 shown for change. The CRDC covers the 50 states, the District of Columbia, and Puerto Rico; American Samoa, Guam, the Northern Mariana Islands, and the U.S. Virgin Islands are not collected by the CRDC and do not appear. Districts report how many students were physically restrained, mechanically restrained, or secluded at least once, and how many incidents occurred; students and incidents are different measures and are labelled separately throughout. One student can be counted in more than one practice type, so a combined student figure is a sum across types — the sum of the three type counts, in which a student reported in two types is counted twice — and is never a count of unique students. Every combined figure, rate, and ratio on this page is labelled on that basis where it appears. District figures are the sum of the district's schools. Rates are students reported per 1,000 enrolled, which is what makes a 400-student district comparable to a 40,000-student one.

A reported zero is a report, not a verified fact. Districts self-report, and this page does not independently verify district reports. In a review of the 2015–16 collection, the U.S. Government Accountability Office found that some of the nation's largest districts reported zeros that were incorrect (GAO-19-551R, June 2019); the GAO made no finding about any district's 2023–24 report. In 2023–24, 64% of districts reported zero — including 148 districts enrolling more than 10,000 students each. This page therefore shows three states distinctly: reported 1 or more students, reported zero students, and no valid report in the CRDC file. State definitions and counting rules differ, so cross-state gaps reflect reporting practice as well as practice itself.

Small numbers and special settings. Rates for very small districts swing on a handful of students; rankings use minimum-enrollment thresholds (1,000 nationally, 500 within states) and every district above 100 students reported per 1,000 is shown with a context note instead of being ranked. Districts that operate therapeutic, hospital, or juvenile-justice programs — including programs serving students placed by other districts — will show high rates that describe their enrollment, not necessarily their conduct.

2020–21 is a pandemic year. Widespread virtual instruction mechanically lowered 2020–21 counts (many virtual-only schools were skipped for these items). Change figures are computed only for districts that reported in both years and should be read as “reported change” — part real change, part return to in-person schooling, part change in counting.

Methodology notes: CRDC reserve codes (−3, −5, −6, −9, −12, −13) are treated as missing, never as counts. A school that reported a practice type with no counts is scored zero for that type; a school that did not report stays missing — that is what keeps “reported zero students” and “no valid report in the CRDC file” distinct. District rates and ratios are computed from the summed counts, never by averaging school rates. The enrollment denominator is enrollment as summed from schools in the CRDC file, cross-checked against the district's own LEA-reported total.
